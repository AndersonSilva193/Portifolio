# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: small-business owners in Brazil (food service, local retail, services such as barbershops) looking for someone to build a site or landing page that sells. They usually arrive from a WhatsApp link, Instagram or a referral, on a phone, and decide in a few seconds whether to send a message.

Secondary: recruiters and tech leads hiring for Java back-end roles (CLT or internship). For them the landing pages prove care and delivery; the Java/Spring work proves engineering.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio of Anderson Silva, developer in Curitiba/PR. Its job is to turn a visitor into a WhatsApp conversation about a site, and secondarily into a recruiter download of the resume. Success: the visitor sees work that looks as good as the sites themselves, and taps WhatsApp.

## Positioning

Anderson builds landing pages with a distinct art direction per business (each project has its own visual world, not a template) and he is also a Java back-end developer, so he can deliver the system behind the page (scheduling, orders, APIs), deployed with Docker on a real domain. A template-site seller cannot truthfully claim the back-end half; a back-end-only dev cannot show the art direction.

## Operating Context

Visitors open the link on a phone first. Contact happens on WhatsApp (`5541984018011`). Recruiters open it on desktop and want the resume PDF, GitHub and LinkedIn.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static site: plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JS, no build step, deployed to GitHub Pages from `main` (andersonsilva193.github.io/Portifolio/). Third-party JS only via pinned CDN.
- Portuguese (pt-BR) copy.
- Light/dark toggle exists today; keeping it is not a stated requirement.

## Product Principles

1. The work leads. Each project is shown in its own visual world, at a size where its craft is visible.
2. Never claim more than is true: client vs. concept is always labeled.
3. One action: talk on WhatsApp. Resume and GitHub are secondary paths for recruiters.
4. The portfolio itself must be built at the level of the sites it sells.
